Analog Harmonic Tremolo

A two-band guitar effect that modulates low and high frequencies in opposition using optically controlled gain.

Overview

01

Frequency-dependent amplitude modulation, entirely in analog hardware.

The project explores harmonic tremolo as an electroacoustic system rather than a single broadband amplitude control. The input is separated into two bands, whose levels are alternately reduced to create movement in both amplitude and spectral balance.

The system was organized into two main sections:

  1. A control circuit consisting of a low-frequency oscillator (LFO), a depth control stage, a gain stage, and a phase splitter that generates two opposing control signals.
  2. An audio circuit that divides the guitar signal into low-pass and high-pass branches, controls the attenuation of each band through LED–LDR optocouplers, and finally recombines both branches.

System architecture

Two paths, one coordinated effect. (MAS NEUTRO)

CTRL Control path
LFO→Depth→Gain→Phase splitter

Produces two opposing low-frequency control signals for the optocoupler LEDs.

AUDIO Audio path
Input→LP / HP split→LED–LDR gain→Sum

Separates the guitar signal, modulates each band independently, and passively recombines both branches.
