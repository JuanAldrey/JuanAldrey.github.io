Overview

01

A new effect, a broader design process

The harmonic tremolo gave me hands-on experience with assembly, testing, and experimental adjustments. I enjoyed that work enough to take on another guitar effect and explore the tools needed to develop a circuit into a compact printed circuit board.

This project was developed with Leonardo Sandez for Electronic Circuits II at UNTREF. We designed a delay with adjustable delay time, repeats, wet-signal level, and bass and treble controls. The work covered circuit analysis, frequency-response simulation, and a four-layer PCB layout in Altium.

The PT2399 handles the digital delay, while the surrounding analog stages condition, filter, and mix the signal. The result documented here is a circuit and PCB design, with simulated responses and 3D board views.

Circuit architecture

02

Digital delay, analog signal conditioning

The circuit was organized into five stages: power supply, input buffer, PT2399 delay, tone control, and output summer. An external 9 V supply feeds the analog stages; a regulator supplies 5 V to the PT2399, and a 4.5 V reference biases the single-supply op-amps.

The input buffer feeds both the dry path and the delay branch. Filtering around the PT2399 conditions the signal before and after digital processing. Feedback sets the repetitions, the tone network shapes the delayed signal, and the output stage combines it with the dry signal.

Simulation

03

Checking how the circuit shapes the sound

Frequency-response simulations were used to evaluate the bass and treble controls at their extreme positions. The design targeted characteristic frequencies of 600 Hz and 800 Hz, with approximately 12 dB of boost or cut.

The calculated frequencies were 626.97 Hz and 822.08 Hz. The plots below show output voltage for a 1 V peak input: an output near 4 V corresponds to approximately 12 dB of gain. These responses connect component choices to the tonal range available to the player.

PCB design

04

From schematic to a 5 × 5 cm board

The schematic was developed into a compact PCB in Altium Designer. Surface-mount components were selected to fit the circuit onto a 5 × 5 cm board, with connections for the signal input, output, power supply, and external controls.

The four-layer layout uses the top and bottom layers for signal routing, one internal layer as a ground plane, and another to distribute the supply voltages. Test pads expose the supply rails, and dedicated connections at the tone-control input and output support future frequency-response measurements.

This was the main extension of my earlier pedal work: developing the circuit into a board layout and inspecting its routing, layer structure, and component placement through Altium’s 2D and 3D views.
